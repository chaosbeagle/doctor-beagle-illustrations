# QA Checklist

## Must Pass

- 16:9 horizontal image.
- Clean pure white background.
- Doctor Beagle appears.
- Doctor Beagle performs the core conceptual action, not decoration.
- Doctor Beagle matches the reference sheets: short legs, compact body, floppy tan ears, tiny black eyes, black oval nose, white muzzle/belly, tan patches.
- Fresh metaphor for the current article, not a copied old composition.
- Strange, clever, and memorable.
- Clean and spacious; main subject not over about 60% of the canvas.
- One image explains one core structure.
- Labels are short, sparse, readable, and in Traditional Chinese or English as appropriate.
- Orange only marks the main path/thread/arrow/action.
- Red only marks emphasis, problems, warning, or result.
- Blue only marks secondary notes, feedback, or system state.

## Failure Signals

Regenerate or edit when:

- A top-left title appears, such as "Workflow", "System Map", "Common Pitfalls", or "Roadmap".
- Simplified Chinese appears when the user did not ask for it.
- Doctor Beagle looks like a realistic dog, emoji, sticker, plush toy, child mascot, or commercial logo.
- Doctor Beagle loses the beagle traits or changes markings too much.
- Doctor Beagle is just watching from the side.
- The image looks like PPT, a course slide, formal flowchart, or dense explainer.
- Too many nodes, arrows, labels, or objects.
- Text becomes long explanations.
- Background has texture, shadow, gradient, beige tone, or noise.
- Real UI screenshot or sci-fi tech interface appears.
- The image is rigid and lacks a strange physical metaphor.

## Iteration

- Too ordinary: make Doctor Beagle perform a stranger low-tech action.
- Too complex: remove nodes; keep one object, one action, and 3-5 labels.
- Too cute: emphasize deadpan, serious, not mascot, not sticker.
- Too PPT-like: remove title, boxes, formal grid, and excess arrows; turn it into a sparse hand-drawn scene.
- Character drift: re-use the visual anchors and restate the exact beagle traits.
- Bad text: edit locally if minor; regenerate with fewer labels if severe.

## Delivery Test

A strong image should feel a little strange at first glance, then become clear within one second.

If it looks like a tutorial slide instead of a strange blank-paper product sketch, it fails.
